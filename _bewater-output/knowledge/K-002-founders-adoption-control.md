## Question or hypothesis

Do time-starved founders and creators actually want an "only think + appear" autonomous workflow, and will they cede editing and distribution control to an AI agent versus keeping a review-before-publish gate?

## Method and scope

Social and review discourse analysis over public sources (2025-2026), Jobs-to-be-Done framing of the adopt-versus-control tension, evidence-strength transferability check. Exclusions: behavioral validation and interview campaigns (require L4 dogfooding). Single wave; directional signal only.

## Sources used

- Reddit r/aipromptprogramming, "What's the BEST AI Tool for Social right now?" — community.
- SolveigMM, "How is AI Redefining Creative Control in Video Editing" — independent-industry.
- YouTube, "AI Content Automation: The Shortcut To Killing Your Brand" — creator-discourse.
- Reddit r/SideProject, "Creators keep saying content takes too long" — community.
- Roan Weigert, "AI Video Editing Transformed Traditional Workflows" — independent-industry.
- Glean, "The Best AI Video Editing Tools for Marketers in 2025" — independent-industry.

## Summary

Creator discourse shows a consistent "trust but verify" posture. The pull toward automation is strong and real: small creators report a single short video eating 2-3 hours between filming, editing, and posting (r/SideProject), and founders are urged to stop editing Reels by hand while automating only the bottom of the funnel. But the willingness to fully cede control is low: creators could not find a single "do-it-all" tool producing brand-ready video automatically, and "one-click" solutions "still fall short on quality and brand alignment" (r/aipromptprogramming); fully automated workflows are seen as risking brand authenticity ("Killing Your Brand," YouTube); and the dominant framing is AI as an assistant for repetitive tasks (subtitles, effects, audio), not as a replacement for creative judgment (SolveigMM, Glean). AI is positioned as "a tool, not a replacement" across the discourse.

## Conclusion

Partial. Directional public evidence supports that founders and creators want AI to remove the editing-time burden but are unwilling to fully cede brand-voice and creative control to an autonomous pipeline; the accepted pattern is AI handles repetitive work with a human review gate before publishing. This supports the Charter's review-before-publish compromise as aligned with market sentiment. The evidence is discourse-level and self-selected; real adoption and the exact control boundary require behavioral validation. Confidence: medium on direction; low on magnitudes.

## Limitations and new questions

Discourse is self-selected and Anglophone; no T1/T2 Chinese-founder signal was retrieved, and no behavioral or interview data exists. The precise control boundary (which decisions founders will delegate versus retain) is unresolved. New questions: in a self-first dogfooding cycle, which editing/distribution decisions does the founder actually override, and how often — the answer defines the product's autonomy surface.
